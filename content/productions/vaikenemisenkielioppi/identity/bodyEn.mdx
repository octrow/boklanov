"The Grammar of Silence" is a visual performance dedicated to the experiences of people belonging to gender and sexual minorities in Russia from the 2010s to the present. It is based on conversations with members of the LGBTQ+ community living in Russia and leaving the country — people whose lives, identity, intimacy, and visibility are gradually becoming increasingly dangerous.

Since the 2010s, the systematic suppression of LGBTQ+ identities in Russia has led to a situation where mere existence can be a crime: public self-expression, communities, and symbols are criminalized, leading to arrests, exile, and silencing.

But what happens in the silence? What remains unsaid to friends, family, colleagues — and to oneself?

The piece has no traditional plot, and the interviews are not told as such on stage. The moods, memories, and internal conflicts captured in the interviews are transformed into a visual collage: objects, materials, puppets, shadows, and the physical expressions of the actors. Words recede, but emotions become visible.

"The Grammar of Silence" does not seek to explain Russia through political slogans or offer the audience ready-made emotions. It invites us to look at people as individuals: to listen to the silence, recognize the mechanisms of fear, and reflect on the freedom we take for granted in our lives.
